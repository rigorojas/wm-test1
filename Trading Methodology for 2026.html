Stock options are derivative financial contracts that give the holder the right, but not the obligation, to buy or sell an underlying asset (like a stock) at a specified price (the strike price) within a specified time period. These contracts derive their value primarily from the price movement of the underlying stock, making them complex tools heavily used in financial markets.

The two primary types of options are Calls and Puts. A Call option grants the holder the right to buy the stock at the agreed-upon strike price, betting that the market price will rise above that level. Conversely, a Put option grants the holder the right to sell the stock at the strike price, which is typically used when a trader anticipates a downward price movement.

Traders utilize options for various strategies, including pure speculation to maximize returns from predicted price movements, or for hedging existing portfolios to mitigate risk. The complexity lies in managing time decay, as all options expire, meaning that the value of the contract diminishes as its expiration date approaches.
