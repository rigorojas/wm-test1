Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. - Life Outline

I. Early Life and Family Background

  • Birth: January 15, 1929, Atlanta, Georgia. Born Michael King Jr., renamed Martin after father's trip to Germany
  • Family: Reverend Martin Luther King Sr., Alberta Williams King, older sister Willie Christine, younger brother A.D.
  • Upbringing in Black middle class, Ebenezer Baptist Church, segregation in Jim Crow South
  • Influences: Parents, church, early experiences with racism

II. Education

  • Atlanta public schools, skipped 9th grade
  • Morehouse College, B.A. in Sociology 1948, age 19
  • Crozer Theological Seminary, B.D. 1951
  • Boston University, Ph.D. in Systematic Theology 1955, dissertation on Paul Tillich and Henry Nelson Wieman

III. Early Ministry and Family Life

  • Pastor at Ebenezer Baptist Church 1947-1960, co-pastor with father
  • Pastor at Dexter Avenue Baptist Church, Montgomery, Alabama 1954
  • Marriage to Coretta Scott 1953, children Yolanda, Martin III, Dexter, Bernice
  • Integration of Christian theology with social justice

IV. Emergence as Civil Rights Leader

  • Montgomery Bus Boycott 1955-1956
    • Rosa Parks arrest Dec 1 1955
    • Organization of boycott, King elected president of Montgomery Improvement Association
    • Bus segregation declared unconstitutional in Browder v. Gayle 1956
  • Bombing of home, threats, commitment to nonviolence

V. National Leadership

  • Founding of Southern Christian Leadership Conference SCLC 1957
  • Role as president and chief strategist
  • Strategies: nonviolent direct action, mass demonstrations, economic pressure, moral suasion

VI. Major Campaigns

  • Birmingham Campaign 1963
    • Project C, Children's Crusade, Bull Connor's police dogs and fire hoses
    • Letter from Birmingham Jail April 16 1963
  • March on Washington for Jobs and Freedom Aug 28 1963
    • "I Have a Dream" speech, 250,000 attendees
  • St. Augustine, Florida 1964
  • Selma to Montgomery Marches 1965
    • Bloody Sunday March 7 1965
    • Voting Rights Act 1965

VII. Philosophy and Key Writings

  • Nonviolent resistance influenced by Gandhi, Tolstoy, Thoreau, Reinhold Niebuhr
  • Core beliefs: Beloved Community, agape love, justice over mere legalism
  • Major speeches and writings: "I Have a Dream", "Letter from Birmingham Jail", "The Other America" 1967

VIII. Expansion of Movement

  • Nobel Peace Prize 1964, youngest recipient at 35
  • Opposition to Vietnam War 1967, "Beyond Vietnam" speech
  • Poor People's Campaign 1968, demand for economic justice
  • Criticism from both white establishment and more militant activists

IX. Assassination and Legacy

  • Assassination April 4 1968, Memphis Tennessee while supporting sanitation workers strike
  • Assassinated by James Earl Ray, national mourning and riots
  • Legacy: Civil Rights Act 1964, Voting Rights Act 1965, Fair Housing Act 1968
  • Influence on subsequent social movements, national holiday established 1983

X. Themes for Essay Expansion

  • Role of faith and preaching in leadership
  • Evolution from local pastor to national symbol
  • Tensions between nonviolence and Black Power
  • Personal costs and family impact
  • Long-term impact on American democracy and global human rights
